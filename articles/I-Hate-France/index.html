I Hate France


On the night of the 17th of October 2026, the Limoges town hall was set ablaze amid a slew of protests happening all across the country of France. These protests in particular have been fuelled primarily by discontent towards the government from French adolescents disappointed about the quality of schooling at secondary schools across France. While the majority of these protests have been peaceful, they cause disruption by allowing bad-faith actors to burn and loot, weakening the perception of France as a stable country with much to offer.

But these protests aren't in response to an unusual cut in benefits or a hike in taxes, they have not been caused by any recent action of the French government. They are just a natural outcome of what happens when you continuously reward protests with concessions. France has trapped itself in a dangerous cycle, ensuring that disruption will continue until spending increases, making their economy weaker with every tax that is cut and benefit that is passed.

The populist sentiment toward current budget issues on one side is to "tax the rich". This sentiment is expressed by far-left figures such as Jean-Luc Melenchon while he pushes against the very idea of economic concepts that critiques of the French economy are based upon by saying: "I get in a car, I hit a wall, GDP goes up because we have to rebuild the wall and fix the car plus the GDP from pharmaceuticals, GDP is nothing, it's a monetary valuation." This may seem like an apt argument about the inaccuracies of the measure of GDP but in reality he is not proposing a slightly adjusted view of the economy. He is trying to truly detach from the economic reality, saying that none of the figures really matter while ignoring the facts leads to the decaying of your country.

On the other side the populist argument doesn't concern itself with critiques of the economic structure, it recognises discontent in French society and focuses it towards Islam and foreign concepts in general, pushing France to be more ideologically insular, with Marine Le Pen saying: "Just watch the interlopers from all over the world come and install themselves in our home. They want to transform France into a giant squat." This push towards a more insular France seems to fit in with the French principle of "fraternite" which makes it popular among the people, but isolation has been proven time and time again to weaken economies and there is great cultural worth in not only having a variety of opinions but also a variety of backgrounds in your country.

The French attitude is unsustainable, it always aggressively pushes for change that is in it's short term best interest even if it has severe consequences in the long run. There are reasons that countries allow the government to make choices rather than trying to influence their every action in a damaging way.

Regardless of who leads France in the future, France needs a cultural shift centered in reality towards allowing elected officials to cut benefits and spending without disruptive protests that lead to the destruction of official buildings and historical landmarks. But the French people show no willingness to waver and are emboldened by pushback, both from the police where a small minority of public order units have mistreated protesters and from people online who the French view as detached from the French struggle and irrelevant in the conversation as it's not their problem. While many countries suffer from time to time, it is often as a result of temporary or uncontrollable events or corrupt leaders while in France it is a dangerous cycle perpetuated by people running their country into the ground as they believe it is for the greater good.

If France continues to operate in the way that it does there will come a point when instead of small austerity measures to create a sustainable society, France will have to undergo massive cuts and will not be able to borrow from other countries. France won't just drag itself down:
  1. It will try to saddle on low quality debt to its European neighbours.
  2. It will massively disrupt the value of the euro, calling into question the modern eurozone experiment.
  3. It will continue to cannibalise itself, making it default on its debt multiple times over.
